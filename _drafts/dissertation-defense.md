# Getting my Ph.D.: a nearly twelve-year journey

## Where things got started

I was what we like to call a non-traditional student. I was not especially studious in high school and was otherwise focused on skateboarding, playing guitar, going to shows, and spending time with friends. I initially went to college largely because I would get to share an apartment with my older brother in Buffalo, New York, where he had already established a friend group. That was in 2011.

I mostly floundered in my first year at the University at Buffalo. I was undeclared and explored the usual liberal-arts courses. At one point, I was absorbed by the American Civil War and considered becoming a historian after taking a course on it, but I knew that would considerably narrow my post-undergraduate prospects. At another point, I thought about digital photography, only to realize I did not yet understand digital art. What seemed certain was that I had no interest in STEM. This was reinforced after nearly failing the only life-science course I took at UB: evolution. “Hardy Wein-who?” “Allele frequencies? What does that mean?”

I have a vivid and bizarre memory of when my interests started to change. It was the summer after my first year at UB. I was at my mother’s house and had just finished watching David Lynch’s *Eraserhead*. Looking for something lighter, I turned on the television and saw an advertisement for organic dog food. A golden retriever pranced through a cornfield in slow motion while a soothing voice described the product’s “all-natural” and organic ingredients. Something about that commercial struck me: “Organic? What does that mean?”

Apparently, I was lost enough in my studies that a dog-food commercial was enough to inspire me to enroll in an environmental-writing course that fall. This was when I began to feel an earnest interest in my studies. In the spring, I enrolled in an environmental-studies course that functioned partly as a book club, reading authors such as Bill McKibben and watching many TED talks. Our final project was to design and present an environmental agency for a cause of our choosing. While searching for ideas, I learned about the “Mushroom Death Suit.” I had always had an interest in death, and with my newfound interest in environmental protection, I discovered that fungi were beautifully positioned at the interface of life and death.
